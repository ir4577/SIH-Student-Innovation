Named for a student who mastered his craft by watching closely.

Eklavya is an AI-powered technique analysis platform. It watches badminton footage the way a coach would — tracking the body, timing the shot, and turning what it sees into a technique score and specific feedback.

What it does today

The current prototype supports badminton only. Upload a clip, tell Eklavya what shot to look for (or let it detect one automatically), and it returns a technique score, a breakdown across six mechanical checkpoints, a shot timeline, and coaching notes on what to keep and what to change.

Other sports — tennis, cricket, football — are on the roadmap but not yet supported.

How the analysis works

Underneath, Eklavya's vision engine is built on OpenCV and MediaPipe's Pose Landmarker. It processes footage frame by frame, tracking 33 body landmarks and calculating elbow and knee joint angles to reconstruct the shape of every shot.

01Frame extraction and pose landmark detection across the full clip.
02Joint angle calculation at the elbow and knee through the swing.
03Movement-peak detection to isolate individual shots.
04Shot classification into smash, drop, drive, or push/block.

Demo Mode vs. a connected backend

The analysis backend today runs as local Python scripts, not yet a hosted API. Whenever this frontend can't reach a live backend, it runs in Demo Mode: your real video is accepted and played back, but the analysis shown is realistic sample data, clearly labeled as a Demo Analysis. The interface never claims a real AI analysis happened when it didn't.

Once the backend is exposed as a REST API, only the request layer needs to change — the rest of this interface is already built against that contract.
